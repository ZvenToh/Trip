01 / ENTRY

The paperwork that can stop you boarding

Get this wrong and nothing else matters. Malaysia's position differs from what most travel content says, and the rules shift again nine days before you fly.

Read this first

You need a K-ETA — for two independent reasons

One: Korea suspended the K-ETA for 67 countries, but Malaysia was never on that exemption list. Singapore, Brunei, Japan, Taiwan, Hong Kong and most Western countries are exempt. Malaysia, Indonesia, the Philippines, Thailand and Vietnam are not.

Two: that exemption expires 31 December 2026 — nine days before you land. Unless Korea extends again, everyone needs one in January 2027. A 2026 blog saying "no K-ETA needed" was never about Malaysia and lapses before your trip.

STEP BY STEPApplying, start to finish

Apply at k-eta.go.kr or the official K-ETA app. This matters more than it sounds: third-party sites charge USD 50–100 for what officially costs about ₩10,000 (roughly RM32). If the domain isn't .go.kr, you're on a reseller. Approval is often quick, but the official guidance is 72 hours before departure — treat that as a hard deadline, not a suggestion.

White background, no glasses, shoulders visible, no head covering unless religious. JPG or PNG, around 700×700 px. A phone photo against a plain wall is fine if the lighting is even and there are no shadows behind you.

You'll need your Korean accommodation address — your first hotel is fine even though you move to Busan later — plus flight numbers and dates for both entry and exit. Have these to hand before you start, because the form times out.

Six months beyond travel is the standard expectation. For a January 2027 trip, a passport expiring before roughly July 2027 should be renewed first — and Malaysian passport renewal takes time, so this is the item to check today rather than later.

Screenshot it rather than relying on email loading at immigration after a red-eye. Approval covers multiple entries and stays valid for years, so it will outlast this trip.

e-Arrival Card — you can skip it

Korea requires an arrival card from most visitors. A quiet benefit of holding an approved K-ETA is that it exempts you from the arrival card — genuinely useful landing at 7am on no sleep. Without one you'd complete the e-Arrival Card online instead.

Length of stay

Malaysians enter visa-free for tourism; the K-ETA replaces a visa rather than adding to one. Nine days sits well inside the short-stay window. Covers tourism, visiting people and events — not paid work.

Recheck before you commit

Entry policy moves faster than anything else here. Korea has extended the exemption several times and may again. Re-check k-eta.go.kr around 2–3 months out — late enough to be accurate, early enough to fix problems.

02 / FLIGHTS

Four ways to route this trip

Your trip starts in Seoul and ends in Busan, which opens routings most people never price. The news that changes things: AirAsia X resumed direct Kuala Lumpur–Busan flights in June 2026 — four weekly, Mon/Wed/Fri/Sun, and the only Malaysian carrier flying it nonstop. Your departure, 17 January, is a Sunday.

03 / PACKING

Dressing for −9 °C when you live at 30 °C

Seoul runs roughly −9 °C to 2 °C in January; Busan is milder but windier. From Kuala Lumpur this is a real shock. The mistake almost everyone makes is buying one enormous coat — Korean interiors are heated hard, so you roast in shops and freeze outside. Thin layers you can shed are the entire strategy.

Buy there, not here

Shorts, sandals, thin cotton anything, most of what you'd wear in KL. Skip a large umbrella too — January in Korea is dry rather than wet, and snow is better handled by a hood.

04 / ACCOMMODATION

Real places, filtered

Rebuilt for your Myeong-dong base. Every property is real, with its actual Google rating, review count and what guests say about the rooms. One honest warning up front: Myeong-dong's budget stock is noticeably weaker than Hongdae's — there are more poorly-run places here trading on the location, so read recent reviews before booking anything not listed below.

On laundry specifically

Almost no Korean hotel room has an in-room washing machine — that's an apartment feature, not a hotel one. You're choosing between coin laundry on site, paid per-item service, or nothing. Your move made this harder: the Hongdae options included places with confirmed coin laundry, whereas in Myeong-dong the properties advertising laundry are mostly the badly-reviewed ones — Migliore has a laundry room but a 3.3★ record, and New Stay Inn charges ₩2,000 wash plus ₩2,000 dry on just two machines, available only 18:00–23:00. Better plan: use a 24-hour coin laundry nearby (several around Myeong-dong and Euljiro), or book an Airbnb filtered for "washer".

What changes by basing here

Myeong-dong trades nightlife for centrality — and mostly in your favour

Better: Seoul Station is one stop away, which makes the Day 6 KTX morning far easier. Dongdaemun is four stops up Line 4, so the Day 2 night ends with a ten-minute ride home. The palace quarter, Euljiro, Namdaemun Market and the Namsan cable car are all walkable or nearly so. Street food is outside your door every evening.

Worse: you're no longer on the airport train line, so arrival needs the limousine bus or a transfer at Seoul Station. Hongdae becomes a 30-minute excursion rather than home, which is why Day 5 was rebuilt. And Myeong-dong is touristy and quieter late — shops shut and the crowd thins, where Hongdae ran all night. Euljiro next door is the fix for that.

Busan: one base or two?

One base, and it should be Seomyeon — the maths changed with the itinerary

Count which days actually want which side. Day 6 arrives at Busan Station (west), stops at Seomyeon (centre), then runs east. Day 7 is entirely east. Day 8 is entirely west. Day 9 is a 07:00 airport run to the northwest. So only one of your four Busan days genuinely favours Haeundae — Day 7.

Why Haeundae for all three nights is the weakest option. It creates a zigzag on Day 6: Busan Station → out east to drop bags → back west to Seomyeon → east again. It adds roughly an hour on Day 8, since Gamcheon is ~1h10 away and you'd return from Nampo at night across the whole city. And it makes Sunday's airport run 60–70 minutes at six in the morning.

Why Seomyeon works for all three. Ten minutes from Busan Station, so you drop bags immediately and the Day 6 Seomyeon stop is literally on your doorstep. ~30 min to Haeundae, ~20 to Nampo, ~40 to Gamcheon and Songdo, and ~33 minutes to Gimhae — the shortest airport run of any major area. It's also cheaper and better for food than Haeundae.

The one real cost, stated plainly: Day 7. From Seomyeon, Blueline Park is ~40 min rather than a walk, and Haedong Yonggungsa becomes about 1h15 instead of 40 minutes. If your Sky Capsule slot is early, that's an early start. Across the whole day you lose perhaps 40–50 minutes — less than Haeundae costs you on Days 6, 8 and 9 combined.

When to split instead (2 Haeundae + 1 Seomyeon/Nampo): only if waking up by the sea for two mornings is worth something to you in itself. On travel time alone it roughly breaks even once you count the checkout, the cross-city bag haul on Day 8, and the extra booking — so it's a preference call, not an efficiency one.

DAY 1 · SATURDAY 9 JANUARY · JONGNO & MYEONG-DONGLand, a UNESCO palace, then home turf

The one hard deadline in your whole trip

Secret Garden tickets open at 10:00 KST, exactly six days before

Only 100 people per tour session: 50 online, 50 sold on the day at the ticket office. Online booking opens on the official Royal Palaces reservation site at 10:00 Korea time, 6 days ahead — so for 9 January that's 10:00 KST on 3 January, which is 09:00 in Malaysia. Slots go fast; one traveller described logging on at the opening minute and finding everything gone.

Practical notes: the palace and the garden are separate tickets — ₩3,000 general admission plus ₩5,000 for Huwon, and the garden ticket alone won't get you through the front gate. Pay within 24 hours of booking or the reservation auto-cancels. The mobile ticket must be live on your phone — a screenshot is not accepted — and it activates 30 minutes before your tour. If online sells out, the 50 same-day tickets are first-come at the ticket office; arriving before opening gives you a real chance on a January Saturday.

Money-saver worth knowing

You're visiting Gyeongbokgung on Day 2 as well. The Royal Palace Pass at ₩10,000 covers Changdeokgung including the Secret Garden, plus Gyeongbokgung, Changgyeonggung, Deoksugung and Jongmyo Shrine, valid three months. Buying separately costs ₩8,000 + ₩3,000 = ₩11,000, so the pass is cheaper and opens up two more palaces if you want them. You still need to reserve a Huwon tour slot — the pass doesn't skip that queue.

The shape: you arrive centre-west at Busan Station, go straight out east for Days 6–7, then sweep back west on Day 8 — which lands you near Gimhae for the 10:00 flight. Haeundae is ~60–70 min from the airport; Nampo, Songdo and Gamcheon are ~40; Seomyeon ~33.
What the maps tell you

Seoul is a hub-and-spoke. Every day starts and ends at Myeong-dong, and no spoke is long. That's the argument for one hotel.

Busan is a line. West at arrival, far east for two days, back west at the end. That's the argument for either splitting your nights or basing centrally in Seomyeon — the one zone that touches everything.

Paying — near-cashless

Cards work at nearly every shop, café and most street stalls in tourist areas. Bring a low-foreign-fee card as primary and carry some won as backup — market stalls at Gukje and BIFF Square are often cash-only.

Cash — look for "Global ATM"

Standard Korean ATMs frequently reject foreign cards. Machines marked Global ATM are common in convenience stores and stations. WOWPASS is a prepaid tourist card that doubles as T-money.

T-money — buy on arrival

Any convenience store or airport booth. A subway ride is about ₩1,400, and it works on buses in both cities plus most convenience stores. The most useful small purchase you'll make.

Tipping and tax

Tipping isn't practised and can confuse people — service is in the price. Going the other way, foreign visitors can claim tax refunds: keep receipts, look for Tax Free signage, process at the airport.

The two costs people underestimate

Fine dining and shopping. Gangnam chef restaurants run past ₩100,000 a head with drinks — Bistro Notre alone requires a ₩39,000 amuse-bouche and a bottle of wine per table before you order. And this route passes three Olive Young flagships, Doota, Dongdaemun and Cheongdam. Set a shopping ceiling before you go.

The catch that affects your trip specifically

Data-only eSIMs have no Korean number — and some Korean apps demand one

Most tourist eSIMs are data-only. Fine for maps, chat and browsing, but some Korean services require a local number for SMS verification at sign-up, and restaurant booking apps are among them. Your whole chef-restaurant plan runs on CatchTable.

Do this: create and test a CatchTable account before committing to an eSIM. If it accepts your Malaysian number, take the eSIM. If it insists on a Korean one, a physical tourist SIM is worth the extra. Either way, book the key restaurants before you fly.

SK Telecom — widest coverage

Largest network, best rural and island reach. Worth it if you were adding countryside or Jeju; for Seoul and Busan you're paying for coverage you won't use.

KT — the middle

Similar pricing to SKT, solid in cities, and runs tourist bundles pairing a SIM with a transport card — one airport transaction.

LG U+ — budget

Cheapest of the three and perfectly good across Seoul and Busan, which is all this itinerary needs. Priced by speed tier, so 5G costs more.

Read the "unlimited" wording

Most Korean unlimited plans cap high-speed data around 3GB per day, then throttle. Ample for maps and photos. If you'll hotspot a laptop, check the daily cap and hotspot rules specifically.

Recommendation

Buy a 10-day eSIM before you fly (₩35–42,000), install at home, switch on when you land — you walk out connected while everyone else queues. Settle the CatchTable question first. Incheon has free airport WiFi as a fallback.

08 / APPS

Install before you fly

One thing catches nearly every first-timer: Google Maps barely works in Korea. Mapping-data export restrictions mean walking and driving directions are unreliable or missing. You need the Korean alternatives, tested at home rather than at Incheon.

The four that are load-bearing

Naver Map, KakaoTalk, Papago and CatchTable. Everything else is convenience — these four your trip depends on.

ESSENTIAL · MAPS

Naver Map

네이버 지도

The default for getting around Korea. Accurate walking directions, subway routing with exit numbers, bus arrivals, business hours. English mode available. Learning the exit numbers saves cold minutes on the wrong side of a road.

ESSENTIAL · MAPS

KakaoMap

카카오맵

The main alternative. Many prefer its interface, and it occasionally has better data for a given place. Have both — when one gives a strange route, check the other.

ESSENTIAL · MESSAGING

KakaoTalk

카카오톡

Korea's universal messenger. Guesthouses and booking services often communicate through it rather than email, so having it makes you contactable the way Koreans expect.

ESSENTIAL · LANGUAGE

Papago

파파고

Naver's translator, distinctly better than Google Translate for Korean. Camera mode reads menus and signs live — invaluable at Gukje Market and the BIFF Square food stalls. Download offline Korean before you fly.

ESSENTIAL · BOOKINGS

CatchTable

캐치테이블

The platform your chef restaurants use — Doryang, Tian Mi Mi, Nuwa and Bistro Notre all book through it. Set up and test early; see the phone-number issue in Connectivity.

TRANSPORT

Kakao T

카카오 T

Korea's dominant taxi app. Easier than flagging one down and explaining a destination, and it protects you from the unmetered "call van" scam. Useful for Haedong Yonggungsa.

TRANSPORT

Korail Talk

코레일톡

Official Korail app for booking your KTX seat on Day 6 — and Day 9 if you take the Incheon fallback. Book ahead rather than turning up.

TRANSPORT

Subway Korea

지하철

Covers both Seoul and Busan, with first and last train times and transfer-optimised routing. Works offline, which genuinely matters underground.

TICKETS

Klook · Trazy

Discounted advance tickets for BUSAN X the SKY, Songdo Cable Car, tours and passes. Often below gate price and lets you skip a queue in the cold.

MONEY

WOWPASS

Prepaid card for foreign visitors — load foreign currency, spend like a Korean debit card, use it as T-money. Kiosks in major subway stations.

FOOD

Naver reviews · MangoPlate

Korean restaurant discovery runs through Naver, not Google. Reviews and photos on Naver Map are the local source of truth; MangoPlate is an English-friendly second opinion.

OPTIONAL

Visit Busan Pass

Bundled pass over 24 or 48 hours covering X the SKY, Songdo Cable Car and free hanbok rental among ~30 sights. Worth the arithmetic against Days 6–8.

Before you leave Malaysia

Install everything, create the accounts, and test them on home WiFi. Several Korean apps need SMS verification or have awkward English sign-up flows — sorting that at home beats doing it jet-lagged in an arrivals hall. Also download offline Korean in Papago and screenshot your K-ETA approval.
